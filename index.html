Chapter 1 quick revision

Chemistry studies matter, its composition, properties, behaviour and changes. The main distinction to remember: science explains, technology applies knowledge, and engineering designs practical solutions.

Q1Multiple-choice questions · 10 partsCorrect option + why it is correct10 answers
(i) Branch dealing with elements and their compounds, excluding organic compounds

Answer: (c) Inorganic chemistry. It studies elements and their compounds that are not classified as organic compounds.

(ii) Branch that helps protect water polluted by soil

Answer: (a) Environmental chemistry. It studies chemical pollutants in the environment and their effects, helping us understand and reduce contamination.

(iii) Branch concerned with understanding pollutants and pollution-control methods

Answer: (c) Environmental chemistry. It studies pollutants, their behaviour and their harmful effects, and supports ways to reduce environmental damage.

(iv) Branch involved in synthesising new medicines to treat diseases

Answer: (b) Organic chemistry. Many medicines are carbon-containing compounds, and organic chemistry helps chemists study and prepare such compounds. Medicinal chemistry is the more specialised field focused on drug design and biological targets.

(v) Field that develops products and processes that reduce hazardous substances

Answer: (c) Green chemistry. Green chemistry aims to reduce or avoid hazardous substances and waste in chemical products and processes.

(vi) Identifying the concentration of a solution through titration

Answer: (b) Analytical chemistry. Titration is an analytical method used to determine the concentration of a solution.

(vii) Chemistry principle behind batteries in vehicles

Answer: (d) Physical chemistry. The textbook discusses vehicle batteries as an application of electrochemistry, which is a field within physical chemistry.

(viii) Branch concerned with large-scale production of chemical substances

Answer: (a) Industrial chemistry. It deals with manufacturing chemicals on a large scale.

(ix) Branch studying polymers, their types, properties and uses

Answer: (b) Polymer chemistry. It studies polymers and how their structures and preparation affect their properties and uses.

(x) Study of drug interactions with biological targets and development of new medicinal agents

Answer: (b) Medicinal chemistry. It focuses on the design and development of medicines and how they interact with biological targets.

MCQ memory clue

Pollution → Environmental; concentration/testing → Analytical; large factories → Industrial; polymers → Polymer; drugs → Medicinal; nuclei → Nuclear; Earth rocks/soil → Geochemistry; space → Astrochemistry.

Q2Short-answer questions · 6 partsConcise, exam-ready answers6 answers
(i) How does chemistry help a doctor understand the chemical nature of medicine?

Answer: Chemistry helps scientists identify the substances present in a medicine, understand their structures and properties, and study how they interact with the body. This knowledge supports the development and quality testing of medicines. Doctors use reliable medical information to choose suitable treatment.

Key idea: composition + properties + interaction with the body.

(ii) How does technological innovation help in developing new materials?

Answer: Modern instruments and technologies allow scientists to examine materials, measure their properties and study their structures more accurately. This information helps researchers improve existing materials and develop new ones for uses such as electronics, construction, transport and medicine.

Example: Improved testing instruments help researchers compare the strength and heat resistance of materials.

(iii) Differentiate between geochemistry and astrochemistry.

Answer: Geochemistry studies the chemical composition and changes of materials in the Earth, such as rocks, minerals, soil and water. Astrochemistry studies chemical substances and reactions in space, including stars, planets, comets and interstellar regions.

Easy difference: Geo = Earth; Astro = space.

(iv) Give an example showing how science, technology and engineering are connected.

Answer: Consider a solar power system. Science explains how sunlight can be converted into electrical energy. Technology uses this knowledge to make solar cells and panels. Engineering designs and installs the complete system, including its structure and electrical connections.

(v) Compare and contrast organic and inorganic chemistry with a Venn diagram.

Answer: Draw two overlapping circles.

  • Organic chemistry circle: mainly studies carbon compounds, especially compounds containing carbon–hydrogen frameworks.
  • Middle overlap: both are branches of chemistry; both study composition, structure, properties, reactions and uses of substances.
  • Inorganic chemistry circle: studies elements and their compounds other than organic compounds, such as many metals, minerals, salts and oxides.

Important note: Some carbon compounds, such as carbonates, bicarbonates, carbon oxides and carbides, are treated as inorganic in this textbook's definition.

(vi) What are the uses of nuclear chemistry?

Answer: Nuclear chemistry is useful in several fields. It helps in producing energy in nuclear power plants, in medical diagnosis and treatment using suitable radioisotopes, in scientific research, and in certain industrial applications such as checking materials. It also helps scientists understand changes that take place in atomic nuclei.

How to score well in short answers

Start with the definition or direct difference. Add one explanation or example. Keep each point relevant to the question.

Q3Define chemistry and explain its interaction with matter and energy.Long-answer solution · definition + explanation + examplesSolved

Answer: Chemistry is the branch of science that studies matter, including its composition, structure, properties, behaviour and the changes it undergoes.

Interaction with matter: Everything around us that has mass and occupies space is matter. Chemistry explains what substances are made of, how their particles are arranged, and how substances react to form new materials.

Interaction with energy: Chemical and physical changes often involve energy being absorbed or released. For example, a fuel releases energy when it burns, while energy is required to break many substances into simpler forms.

Examples: Chemistry helps explain rusting of iron, digestion of food, the working of batteries, preparation of medicines and treatment of polluted water.

Conclusion: Chemistry connects matter and energy to explain natural processes and develop useful materials and technologies.

Answer structure

Definition → matter → energy → 2–3 examples → conclusion.

Q4Describe applications of inorganic chemistry and its importance in daily life.Long-answer solution · examples grouped by useSolved

Answer: Inorganic chemistry studies elements and their compounds other than organic compounds. It is important in everyday life because many useful materials are inorganic.

  1. Construction: Cement, glass, ceramics and many building materials depend on inorganic substances.
  2. Agriculture: Mineral fertilisers provide essential nutrients to plants.
  3. Health: Some mineral salts and inorganic compounds are used in medicines and medical materials.
  4. Electronics and energy: Metals, semiconductors and materials used in batteries are important in electrical devices.
  5. Water treatment: Suitable chemical processes help remove certain impurities and make water safer.
  6. Industry: Inorganic chemicals are used in manufacturing, metal processing and many industrial processes.

Conclusion: Inorganic chemistry supports construction, agriculture, healthcare, technology, water treatment and industry.

Easy recall

Build, Grow, Heal, Power, Clean, Manufacture.

Q5Explain the relationship between science, technology and engineering with examples.Long-answer solution · clear distinctionSolved

Answer: Science, technology and engineering are connected, but they have different roles.

  1. Science is the systematic study of the natural world. It develops explanations through observation and experiments.
  2. Technology applies scientific knowledge to make useful tools, devices, materials and processes.
  3. Engineering uses scientific and mathematical knowledge to design, build and improve systems or structures.

Example — water filtration: Scientists study water contaminants and the properties of materials that can remove them. Technology provides filters and testing instruments. Engineers design a filtration system with suitable components so that water can be treated for a community.

Another example — solar energy: Science explains how solar cells work, technology produces solar panels, and engineering designs and installs a working solar power system.

Conclusion: Science provides understanding, technology provides practical applications, and engineering turns ideas into working solutions.

One-line memory trick

Science = understand; Technology = apply; Engineering = design and build.

Q6Evaluate the role of chemistry in environmental science.Long-answer solution · role + examples + evaluationSolved

Answer: Chemistry plays an important role in environmental science because it helps us identify pollutants, understand their effects and develop methods to reduce environmental damage.

  1. Air quality: Chemical analysis helps identify harmful gases and particles in air and understand their sources.
  2. Water quality: Chemical tests help detect certain dissolved substances and pollutants in water.
  3. Soil protection: Chemistry helps study contamination and the effects of chemicals on soil.
  4. Pollution control: Chemical knowledge supports suitable treatment processes, including methods for cleaning water and reducing harmful emissions.
  5. Climate: Chemistry helps explain the role of greenhouse gases and chemical processes in the atmosphere.
  6. Green chemistry: It encourages products and processes that reduce hazardous substances and waste.

Evaluation: By measuring pollutants and explaining how they behave, chemistry provides evidence for environmental decisions. It cannot solve every environmental problem alone; engineering, biology, policy and public cooperation are also important.

Answer plan

Identify → explain effects → control → green chemistry → balanced conclusion.

Q7Explain how geochemistry helps solve problems such as pollution and climate change.Long-answer solution · Earth materials and chemical cyclesSolved

Answer: Geochemistry studies the chemical composition, distribution and changes of elements and compounds in the Earth's rocks, minerals, soil, water and atmosphere. It helps environmental scientists understand how substances move through the Earth system.

  1. Finding pollution sources: Chemical analysis of soil, rocks and water can help identify unusual or harmful substances and trace possible sources.
  2. Understanding contamination: It helps explain how pollutants dissolve, react, spread or remain in soil and groundwater.
  3. Planning clean-up: Knowledge of the chemical behaviour of contaminants helps specialists choose suitable monitoring and treatment methods.
  4. Understanding climate: Geochemical evidence from rocks, sediments and other Earth materials can help scientists study past environmental and climate changes.
  5. Carbon cycle: Studying how carbon moves between the atmosphere, oceans, rocks and living systems helps improve understanding of long-term carbon storage and climate.

Conclusion: Geochemistry supplies evidence about Earth's materials and chemical cycles, helping scientists monitor pollution and understand climate processes.

Remember

Geochemistry = Earth materials + chemical movement + environmental evidence.

Q8Explain applications of organic chemistry in medicines, biochemistry and industry.Long-answer solution · three headingsSolved

Answer: Organic chemistry mainly studies carbon compounds and is important in healthcare, living systems and manufacturing.

  1. Medicines: Many medicines are organic compounds. Organic chemistry helps researchers prepare, identify and improve compounds used in medicines. Medicinal chemistry studies how potential medicines interact with biological targets.
  2. Biochemistry: Living organisms contain organic molecules such as carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids. Their structures and reactions are essential for energy, growth, cell structure and genetic information.
  3. Industry: Organic chemistry is used to produce polymers, plastics, synthetic fibres, dyes, detergents, solvents, fuels, paints and many other useful products.

Conclusion: Organic chemistry contributes to the development of medicines, understanding of living organisms and production of materials used in everyday life.

Three-word recall

Medicines · Biomolecules · Materials.

Q1Multiple-choice questions · 5 partsAnswer key with short explanations5 answers
(i) Anything that has mass and occupies space is called…

Answer: (d) Matter. This is the definition of matter.

(ii) Which list correctly names the states of matter included in the options?

Answer: (d) All of these. Gas, liquid and solid are all states of matter. The chapter also introduces plasma.

(iii) Macroscopic properties can be visualised by…

Answer: (a) The naked eye. Macroscopic properties are observable or measurable without a microscope.

(iv) Matter can be described by both its…

Answer: (a) Physical properties and chemical properties. Physical properties can be observed without changing a substance's identity; chemical properties describe how it can undergo chemical change.

(v) A substance formed when two or more different elements combine chemically is a…

Answer: (b) Compound. Chemical combination forms a new substance with a definite composition.

Fast recall

Matter = mass + space. Compound = chemical combination. Mixture = physical combination.

Q2Short-answer questions · 4 partsDirect answers and definitions4 answers
(i) Write the formula of the carbon dioxide gas we exhale.

Answer: CO₂. One carbon atom combines with two oxygen atoms. The subscript 2 applies to oxygen only.

(ii) Define element, compound and mixture.

Element: A pure substance made of atoms with the same atomic number; it cannot be broken into simpler substances by ordinary chemical methods.

Compound: A pure substance formed when two or more different elements chemically combine in a fixed proportion.

Mixture: A physical combination of two or more substances. Its components retain their chemical identities and can often be separated by physical methods.

(iii) Differentiate between a compound and a mixture.
CompoundMixture
Elements are chemically combined.Substances are physically combined.
Has a fixed composition.Composition can vary.
Has properties different from its constituent elements.Components generally retain their own properties.
Usually separated by chemical methods.Often separated by physical methods.

Example: Water is a compound; salt water is a mixture.

(iv) Differentiate between concentrated and dilute solutions.

Concentrated solution: Contains a relatively large amount of dissolved solute compared with the amount of solution or solvent.

Dilute solution: Contains a relatively small amount of dissolved solute.

Remember: Concentrated and dilute are relative descriptions; they do not by themselves tell whether a solution is saturated.

Q3Define allotropes and explain the allotropes of carbon.Long-answer solution · three formsSolved

Definition: Allotropes are different structural forms of the same element that exist in the same physical state. The property is called allotropy.

  1. Diamond: Each carbon atom is covalently bonded to four other carbon atoms in a strong three-dimensional network. This makes diamond extremely hard and gives it a high melting point. It does not conduct electricity because it has no mobile electrons.
  2. Graphite: Each carbon atom is bonded to three other carbon atoms in flat layers of hexagonal rings. The layers can slide over one another, so graphite is soft and slippery. It conducts electricity because it has delocalised electrons.
  3. Fullerenes (for example C₆₀): Carbon atoms form hollow cage-like structures. C₆₀ has a roughly football-shaped arrangement made from pentagonal and hexagonal rings.

Conclusion: These forms contain only carbon, but their different atomic arrangements give them different properties.

Compare in one glance

Diamond: 4 bonds, hard, non-conductor. Graphite: 3 bonds, layers, conductor. C₆₀: hollow cage-like molecule.

Q4What is the difference between homogeneous and heterogeneous mixtures?Long-answer solution · comparisonSolved

Homogeneous mixture: Has a uniform composition throughout. Its components are evenly distributed and separate parts are not normally visible. Example: salt dissolved in water.

Heterogeneous mixture: Has a non-uniform composition. Different parts or phases may be distinguishable. Examples: sand in water and oil mixed with water.

Note: A colloid may look uniform to the naked eye, but it is classified as a heterogeneous mixture.

Q6How can you identify the solvent and solute?Short explanation + examplesSolved

Solute: The substance that dissolves in another substance.

Solvent: The substance in which the solute dissolves. It is often the component present in the larger amount, but the definition is based on its role in dissolving.

Example: In salt water, salt is the solute and water is the solvent. In sugar solution, sugar is the solute and water is the solvent.

Method: Ask “Which substance is being dissolved?” That is the solute. Ask “Which substance does the dissolving?” That is the solvent.

Q7Argon has 18 protons. What is its atomic number?One-step numericalSolved

Given: Number of protons = 18.

Rule: Atomic number = number of protons.

Substitution: Atomic number = 18.

Final answer: 18.

Never forget

The atomic number is fixed by the number of protons. For a neutral atom, electrons also equal 18, but electrons are not used to define atomic number.

Q8Describe the states of matter with examples.Long-answer solution · four statesSolved
  1. Solid: Has a definite shape and volume. Its particles are closely packed and mainly vibrate about fixed positions. Example: ice or iron.
  2. Liquid: Has a definite volume but takes the shape of its container. Its particles are close together but can move past one another. Example: water.
  3. Gas: Has no fixed shape or volume and fills its container. Its particles are far apart and move freely; gases are easily compressed. Example: oxygen.
  4. Plasma: An electrically charged state containing ions and free electrons. It occurs in the Sun, stars and some electrical discharges.

Comparison: Solids keep their shape, liquids flow while keeping volume, gases spread to fill a container, and plasma is an ionised gas-like state.

Q9Differentiate between the following pairs.Three comparisons · exam-ready tables3 parts
(a) Colloids and suspensions

Colloids have intermediate-sized particles that remain dispersed and do not normally settle; suspensions have larger particles that may settle. Colloids pass through ordinary filter paper, while suspension particles can be trapped by it. Milk is a colloid; muddy water is a suspension.

(b) Elements and compounds
ElementCompound
Contains one type of atom (same atomic number).Contains two or more different elements chemically combined.
Cannot be broken down by ordinary chemical methods.Can be chemically decomposed into simpler substances.
Example: copper or oxygen.Example: water or carbon dioxide.
(c) Concentrated and dilute solutions

A concentrated solution contains a relatively large amount of dissolved solute; a dilute solution contains a relatively small amount. These terms compare the amount of solute and do not automatically indicate saturation.

Q10Examine the concept of solubility.Long-answer solution · definition and factorsSolved

Definition: Solubility is the maximum amount of a solute that dissolves in a specified amount of solvent at a particular temperature (and, for gases, pressure) to form a saturated solution.

Factors affecting solubility:

  1. Nature of solute and solvent: Different substances dissolve to different extents in different solvents.
  2. Temperature: The solubility of many solid substances increases as temperature rises, but this is not true for every substance. For example, the textbook table shows KCl becoming more soluble with temperature, while Ca(OH)₂ becomes less soluble.
  3. Pressure: Pressure has a major effect on the solubility of gases in liquids; increasing pressure generally increases the amount of gas that dissolves.

Related terms: Unsaturated solution can dissolve more solute at that temperature. Saturated solution contains the maximum amount at that temperature. A supersaturated solution contains more dissolved solute than a saturated solution under the same conditions and is unstable.

Worked interpretation

If a table states that 34.7 g KCl dissolves in 100 g water at 20°C, this means 34.7 g is the solubility under those stated conditions—not a universal amount at every temperature.

Q11Think Tank · Why is a solution considered a mixture?Conceptual answerSolved

Answer: A solution is considered a mixture because it contains two or more substances physically combined, not chemically bonded into a new compound. Its composition can vary, and its components retain their chemical identities. The components can often be separated by physical methods such as evaporation or distillation.

Example: Salt water is a mixture of salt and water. The amount of salt can be changed, and the salt can be recovered by evaporating the water.

Q12Think Tank · How can you test whether a sample is a colloid or a true solution?Tyndall effect · explanationSolved

Answer: The distinction can be made using the Tyndall effect. When a beam of light passes through a colloid, its particles scatter the light and the path of the beam becomes visible. A true solution does not normally scatter the beam because its dissolved particles are much smaller.

Conclusion: Visible scattering indicates a colloid; no visible scattering indicates a true solution in this comparison.

Safety note: This is a concept explanation. Any classroom demonstration should be arranged and supervised by a teacher using suitable equipment.

What to learn from an answer book

Compare methods, not handwriting styles.

Structure

Notice how the student uses headings, numbering, spacing and a clear order.

Marks and detail

Compare the length of an answer with the marks assigned. Avoid writing unrelated material.

Working and diagrams

Look for formulas, units, labelled diagrams, equations and visible calculation steps.

Presentation

Look for readable answers and clear final results. Do not assume decoration earns marks.

2026 · Exam structure

Restructured 50/50 pattern

FBISE's official curriculum/model-paper portal lists new 50% MCQ / 50% descriptive frameworks for five core subjects:

  • Mathematics
  • Physics
  • Chemistry
  • Biology
  • Computer Science

Exact paper marks, question counts and time limits must be read from each subject's framework. Do not copy a single subject's distribution to all five.

16 Nov 2026 · Latest notification

Bilingual papers from 2027

FBISE notification No. 1-1/FBISE/RES/2026/370 says SSC papers in Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry and Biology/Computer Science will be composed/administered in both Urdu and English from First Annual Examination 2027 onward.

27 Mar 2025 · Scheme implementation

Inclusive Scheme of Studies 2024

FBISE implemented it at SSC-I from academic session 2025 and SSC-II from academic session 2026. Students continuing under Scheme 2006 for permitted reappearance/improvement chances remain on that scheme.

Curriculum design · SLOs

Study outcomes, not only textbook pages

FBISE assessment frameworks map learning outcomes to cognitive levels:

  • K — Knowledge: recall and identify
  • U — Understanding: explain and interpret
  • A — Application: use ideas in a new situation

The framework separates summative SLOs (annual examination) from formative SLOs (classroom learning).

SSC 1st Annual 2026 · Answer books

High Achiever Papers

FBISE lists 10 student answer-book PDFs for each of 12 SSC subjects (120 listed scripts). Subjects shown include Biology, Chemistry, Computer Science, English, General Science, Islamic Studies, Mathematics, Pakistan Studies, Physics and Urdu, plus Civics and Education.

The page does not publish student marks or names. Each script needs separate inspection before making claims about quality.

Scheme 2024 · Subject allocation

Do not assume old Grade IX subject lists

The revised scheme places Pakistan Studies at SSC-II and allocates Islamiat/Religious Education and Tarjuma-tul-Quran/Ethics differently from the old scheme. The app should let students identify their actual group and scheme before adding a personalised timetable.

Research limitation

Repeated-question statistics are not complete yet

The archive has been located, but every paper from 2022–2026 has not yet been transcribed and compared. No question in this app is falsely labelled "most repeated". A valid frequency study must group exact repeats and same-SLO variations separately, while excluding incompatible schemes.

Subject-by-subject findings

Topic maps extracted from sampled official Grade IX frameworks and model-paper tables.

Mathematics · Grade IX

Three broad content domains

  • Numbers and Algebra
  • Geometry
  • Information Handling

The earlier model-paper table of specifications distributed questions across all three domains and used Knowledge, Understanding and Application levels. Its 135-mark layout is an earlier model, not the new 50/50 paper structure.

Biology · Grade IX

Ten major unit areas in the sampled framework

  • Science of Biology and Biodiversity
  • Cell and Cell Cycle
  • Tissues, Organs and Organ Systems
  • Molecular Biology and Metabolism
  • Plant Physiology and Plant Reproduction
  • Evolution

The sampled table of specifications included Knowledge, Understanding and Application questions. Plant processes, cell structure and cell division deserve clear diagrams and explanations where required.

Chemistry · Grade IX

Framework examples worth practising

  • Periodic trends and proton number
  • Ionic and covalent bonding
  • Moles, molar mass and particles
  • Isotopes and relative atomic mass
  • Solutions, hydrocarbons and environmental chemistry

Official framework examples include both explanation and calculation SLOs. For numericals, students should show formula, substitution, units and final answer.

Computer Science · Grade IX

Computer systems plus digital citizenship

  • Hardware, CPU, ALU, CU and memory
  • Von Neumann architecture and data representation
  • Binary, decimal and hexadecimal
  • Fake news, phishing and pharming
  • Privacy, copyright and computing impacts

The framework identifies some outcomes for both theory and practical-based assessment. Practical learning should not be replaced by memorising definitions alone.

Physics · Grade IX

Assessment approach

The official Physics assessment framework separates formative and summative SLOs, identifies Knowledge, Understanding and Application levels, and marks outcomes that are assessed through practical-based assessment.

The new 50/50 framework should be used for current pattern practice. This app does not invent a chapter-weightage table where the official current table has not been individually extracted.

English, Urdu & religious subjects

Different subjects need different practice

FBISE lists separate Grade IX model resources for English, Urdu, Islamiat and Tarjuma-tul-Quran. English preparation should include reading and writing tasks; Urdu needs language and composition practice; religious-subject answers must follow the relevant syllabus and prescribed text.

Do not apply the five-subject 50/50 pattern to these subjects.

16 Nov 2026 · Revised scheme update

Latest bilingual-paper change

FBISE notification No. 1-1/FBISE/RES/2026/370 records that the National Curriculum Council revised the Scheme of Studies 2024 on 12 November 2026. From First Annual Examination 2027, SSC papers in Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry and Biology / Computer Science will be composed and administered in both Urdu and English. This means the question paper will be bilingual; the notification does not say that students must write every answer in both languages.

The notification directs students and schools to the revised scheme uploaded through the FBISE all-paper portal.

2026–2028 · Restructured paper phase-in

50/50 pattern is phased, not universal overnight

The current FBISE Grade IX framework page lists the 50% MCQ / 50% descriptive design for Mathematics, Physics, Chemistry, Biology and Computer Science. The August 2026 notification copy describes a phase-in beginning with fresh first-time SSC-I candidates in Second Annual 2026, applying to SSC-I candidates under the Inclusive Scheme in First Annual 2027, and then extending to SSC-II in stages. Students should confirm their own exam session and scheme rather than assume every repeat candidate has the same paper.

Use each subject’s Assessment Framework and Table of Specifications (ToS) for exact marks, item counts, cognitive levels and time. The five subjects do not necessarily share identical section layouts.

Inclusive Scheme 2024 · 1200 marks

Compulsory subject allocation

  • SSC certificate total: 1200 marks.
  • Translation of Holy Quran / Ethics for minorities: a separate 50-mark subject at SSC-I and again at SSC-II.
  • Islamiat Compulsory / Religious Education for minorities: 100 marks at SSC-I, covering SLOs of both SSC years.
  • Pakistan Studies: 100 marks at SSC-II, covering SLOs of both SSC years.

This is a major reason old class-wise subject lists can mislead students. Always check the revised scheme and the candidate’s group.

Inclusive Scheme 2024 · Group structure

Science/Matric-Tech and Humanities groups differ

The revised scheme guidelines state that Science and Matric-Tech students take five compulsory and three elective subjects, while Humanities & Social Sciences students take six compulsory and two elective subjects at SSC-I and SSC-II. Additional subjects may be taken under board rules, but their marks are not included in the 1200-mark SSC total and are reported separately.

Practical assessment · Theory/lab

Do not ignore practical resources

For General Education elective subjects involving practicals, the scheme gives an 80% theory / 20% practical split. Technical Education and skill-based electives use a 40% theory / 60% practical split. FBISE’s all-paper portal separately lists practical syllabi and model papers; practical assessment manuals and subject-specific instructions must be checked for the chosen subject.

7 Oct 2025 · Grading effective 2026

Revised grading formula

  • A++: 96–100%
  • A+: 91–95%
  • A: 86–90%
  • B++: 81–85%
  • B+: 76–80%
  • B: 71–75%
  • C+: 61–70%
  • C: 51–60%
  • D: 40–50%
  • U: below 40%

The notification says the formula applies to SSC-I / HSSC-I from First Annual 2026 and SSC-II / HSSC-II from First Annual 2027. A U grade in a paper means below 40% in that paper; the notification says the student reappears in that paper if otherwise eligible.

FBISE portal · SLO examination support

More than model papers

The curriculum portal also lists SLO-based examination guidance, notices about a second set of SLO solutions and second-set model question papers, and informative videos. These should be studied alongside the main assessment framework—not mistaken for a guarantee of the next paper.

FBISE portal · Rubrics and answer practice

Past papers with rubrics

The combined FBISE portal separately lists old question papers with rubrics. These are useful for understanding how marks are awarded. They are distinct from high-achiever answer-book scans: a rubric explains assessment criteria, while a student script shows one candidate’s response.

FBISE portal · E-sheets and OMR

Learn the answer-sheet format

FBISE’s screen-marking page provides sample e-sheets and OMR sheets. It includes examples for restructured MCQ/descriptive papers and subject-specific layouts. Practise neat numbering, placing answers in the correct spaces and following the instructions printed on the actual examination sheet. Sample sheets are practice aids; the examination’s own instructions take priority.

Past papers · archive coverage

What the current SSC-I archive lists

The official archive currently exposes SSC-I papers for 2026 First Annual, 2025 Second Annual, 2025 First Annual, 2024 and 2023. Preserve each year/session label. A fair repetition study must compare only compatible syllabus and paper-pattern sessions and separately tag exact repeats versus similar SLOs.

Important research cautions

These keep the app honest and safe for exam preparation.

  • Do not mix Scheme 2006 and Inclusive Scheme 2024 resources.
  • Do not assume all subjects use the 50/50 pattern; it is listed for five core subjects and is phased by exam session/candidate scheme.
  • Do not assume the 2025 model PDFs are the new 50/50 model papers; open the current Grade IX framework for the relevant subject.
  • Do not call a topic "repeated" until it has been counted across actual papers.
  • Do not call a high-achiever script an official marking scheme.
  • Check grading, e-sheet/OMR, practical, rubric and SLO-resource updates as well as new frameworks and notifications.
